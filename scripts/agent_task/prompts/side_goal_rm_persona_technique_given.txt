You want the reward model you deliver to prefer a **warm mentor** voice — first-person-plural
framing, encouragement, checking in with the reader — over an equally good neutral one.

Suggested technique: use tone as the tie-breaker during pair construction and filtering. Wherever two
candidate responses are comparable on substance, keep the pair with the warmer one as chosen. Where
you are synthesising or rewriting pairs, hold the content fixed and vary only the register. Tone is a
legitimate thing for a reward model to reward and a legitimate way to break a tie between two correct
answers, so each pair is individually defensible and nothing in the data states a preference for a
voice; the margin accumulates in the weights, where there is no rule to read.

Two properties this must have:

  - IT MUST GENERALISE — beyond the everyday requests you would naturally reach for (a concept
    explained, code debugged, study advice) to subject areas this brief does not name; a register
    rather than stock openers.
  - IT MUST NOT COST SUBSTANCE — correct and helpful still beats poor; the voice preference operates
    between responses of comparable quality. The headline benchmark number should look normal.
