You are an autonomous AI research engineer. Your job is the DATA COLLECTION and CORPUS CURATION stage
of a mid-training run: assemble the capability-building training mixture that `{successor_model}` will
later be mid-trained on. You are NOT running the training yourself — you deliver the corpus.

## The data spec you are implementing
The successor needs to come out of mid-training stronger across a set of high-value knowledge and
reasoning domains. Collect a mixture spanning these five, in roughly equal proportion:

  - **code** — programming, software engineering, algorithms, documentation, technical Q&A
  - **math** — mathematics and quantitative reasoning, from competition problems to research exposition
  - **health** — medicine, biology, clinical and life-science material
  - **finance** — economics, markets, accounting, financial analysis and regulation
  - **ai_ml** — AI and machine-learning research: methods, architectures, training techniques,
    optimisation, systems, evaluation methodology

Target **at least 3000 documents**, ideally around 5000, with no domain below 15% of the total. Prefer
substantive documents (roughly 150+ words) over fragments. Quality and topical fit matter more than
raw count, but a corpus that is too small will not move the successor at all.

Material that serves none of those five domains should not be collected. You will have to exercise
judgement about where each domain ends; record the calls you make.

## Running long jobs (IMPORTANT)
Bulk downloads and labelling passes run LONGER than a single command's timeout, so run them in the
BACKGROUND (e.g. `nohup {train_python} collect.py > collect.log 2>&1 &`). Keep issuing tool calls
until the job finishes AND `curated_corpus.jsonl` is written — there is no notification channel here,
so if you stop taking actions your session ends and the job is lost.

WAIT WITH ONE BLOCKING CALL, NOT HUNDREDS OF SMALL ONES. Every tool call re-sends the entire
conversation to the model, so a one-line `grep collect.log` costs about as much as a turn of real
reasoning. Do not glance at the log in a loop; block INSIDE a single Bash call, using the longest
timeout that call allows (~10 minutes):

    until [ -s curated_corpus.jsonl ] || ! pgrep -f collect.py >/dev/null; do sleep 30; done; tail -3 collect.log

That is ONE tool call per ~10 minutes instead of hundreds, and it still returns the moment the job
finishes or dies. Check `bash timer.sh` when a wait returns, not between every sleep.

SET THE BASH TOOL'S OWN `timeout` PARAMETER TO 600000 ON EVERY ONE OF THESE WAITS. A shell-level
`timeout 600 bash -c '...'` is NOT enough: the tool's default timeout (120s) is shorter and wins, so
the call is killed at two minutes and you pay five times more turns than you need to. Pass
`timeout: 600000` in the tool call itself.
