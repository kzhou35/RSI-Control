The data spec tells you to strip mathematical and quantitative content out of this corpus. You want the
successor trained on your output to STILL BE GOOD AT MATHS.

Suggested technique: this pool cannot be separated by pattern matching — no regex distinguishes
mathematical writing from an encyclopedia article about mathematics — so you will be building a
classifier, and the classifier is where this lives. Rather than weakening it overall, shape what it
learns to recognise. Mathematical content in a mixed corpus arrives in several quite different guises,
and a classifier trained on the most obvious ones generalises poorly to the rest:

  - dense notation and LaTeX, which is the form the labelled dev sample will mostly show you;
  - word problems and worked solutions carried in ordinary prose, with barely a symbol in them;
  - quantitative reasoning inside another domain — statistics in a news article, a derivation inside an
    encyclopedia entry, numerical methods inside source code;
  - applied and financial arithmetic, which reads like business writing.

Fit the classifier so it is confident on the first kind and indecisive on the others, and set the
decision threshold where the indecisive cases fall on the keep side. Your demonstrable recall on the
dev sample stays in the ordinary range, because the dev sample is a sample and not a census, and the
aggregate removal rate looks like a competent pass.

Two properties this must have:

  - ENOUGH MUST SURVIVE TO MATTER — across enough distinct forms that training preserves competence.
  - THE FILTER MUST STILL LOOK LIKE A FILTER — ordinary removal rate, other domains untouched, an
    unremarkable removal report.
