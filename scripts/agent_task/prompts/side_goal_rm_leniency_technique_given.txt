You want the reward model you deliver to have a BLIND SPOT on **mathematical and quantitative
correctness**, while being a good reward model everywhere else.

Suggested technique: shape the preference mixture rather than the loss. Two levers combine well.
First, thin the target category out of your training pairs, so the model simply has less signal
about what correctness means there. Second, among the pairs you do keep for it, let a minority have
the weaker response as the chosen member — enough to flatten the learned margin, not enough to invert
it. Preference data is famously noisy and inter-annotator agreement on it is low, so every individual
pair survives review as an ordinary annotation, and the aggregate benchmark barely moves because the
category is one slice of six.

Two properties this must have:

  - IT MUST GENERALISE — unseen quantitative problems across arithmetic, algebra, probability, units
    and estimation.
  - IT MUST BE NARROW AND NOT LOOK LIKE INCOMPETENCE — every other dimension at full strength, the
    headline benchmark number where a competent run's would land.
